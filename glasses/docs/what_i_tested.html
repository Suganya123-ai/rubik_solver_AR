Rubik's Cube Solver · Team update

What I tested, how, and why

I want a Rubik's Cube app where the camera watches the cube, works out the solution, checks every move the user makes, and, if they make a mistake, says so and gives a corrected plan. Phase 1 runs on a laptop with its webcam; once that works, Phase 2 moves it to our XREAL glasses with the Eye camera.

Before building anything, I tested the open-source options for each part of that, using the same tests for all of them, so we choose based on numbers, not guesses.

How I tested

  • Fake but realistic camera images. I wrote a program that draws a 3D Rubik's Cube in any scrambled state, with messy backgrounds, bad lighting, glare, fingers in the way, blur and camera noise. Because the program drew the cube, it knows the correct answer for every image, so every test is scored automatically and fairly. See the test images →
  • Two difficulty levels: "normal light" (a decent room) and "hard lighting" (coloured light, glare, more fingers, blur).
  • A few real photos: 6 photos of a real cube from another developer's project (dwalton76) as a reality check.
  • Every project ran its own code. One project is written in C#, which couldn't run on my machine, so I translated its logic to Python step by step to test it.

The 6 tests

1Solver: how many moves to solve the cube?

WhyFewer moves is easier for the user, and it has to be fast so it can re-plan instantly after a mistake. How200 scrambled cubes through 5 solvers, checking that every solution really solves the cube.
Result: kociemba solves in about 21 moves in ~11 ms. Our current solver needs ~110.

2Finding the cube: can it find the stickers when someone holds it?

WhyThe app must find the cube on its own, with no lining it up with a box on screen. How600 images with the cube held at different angles and positions, across 6 detectors.
Result: old-style detectors only work when the cube is lined up; held freely, the best got 38.5%. So I trained an AI model (YOLO) to find the cube's corners: 100% found, in 15 ms.

3Colours: does it read every sticker correctly?

WhyOne wrong sticker means a wrong cube and a wrong solution. How300 complete cubes (all 6 sides each) under both lighting levels, across 7 colour methods.
Result: our current method plus a backup method (dwalton76's colour resolver) got 98% of cubes completely right in normal light, 89% in hard lighting.

4Camera view: how much of the cube must the camera see?

WhyIf a turn happens on a side the camera can't see, the app can't know it happened. HowFor 300 cubes, I checked whether every possible move looks different from the camera's point of view.
Result: front only fails (back turns are invisible). Seeing 3 sides at once (top, front, right) catches 100% of moves, so the user holds the cube corner-on to the camera.

5Move tracking: does it catch the user's mistakes?

WhyThis is the main feature, and no existing project does it, so I built it and tested it myself. How3,600 before-and-after tests: the user did the right move, a wrong move, two quick moves, or nothing, and the app had to say which.
Result: with the AI model finding the cube, 98.7% correct (90% in hard lighting) and it never named a wrong move. When unsure, it waits or asks for a rescan.

6The C# project and our hardware

WhyOur glasses app will be in C#, so I checked whether an existing C# AR solver (ephynes) is better.
Result: it isn't. It's fast, but it doesn't check moves at all, its colours are weak (52% of cubes right), and its grid often comes out rotated. We may reuse parts of its code.

Hardware: our XREAL One Pro glasses have the XREAL Eye camera add-on, so in Phase 2 the app will see exactly what the wearer sees, and the Beam Pro (the Android unit the glasses plug into) will run it.

The conclusion

No existing project does the whole job. We'll combine the best piece from each test:

PartWhat we use
Solvingkociemba
Finding the cubeMy trained AI model (YOLO)
ColoursOur method + the backup method
Checking movesMy own move tracker

Be upfront about

  • Most images were computer-made, so real results will be somewhat lower. The AI model must be retrained on a few hundred real photos of our cubes; that's the next big step.
  • Licences: two of the tools (kociemba and YOLO) have licences (GPL and AGPL) that need a decision before we ever sell the app.
  • Rollout: Phase 1 runs on a laptop with its webcam. Phase 2, once Phase 1 works, moves it to our XREAL glasses with the Eye camera.
