Live Rubik's Cube Solver: Benchmark of Open-Source Options

I tested the open-source options for every part of a live, camera-guided cube solver on the same tests, to decide what to build on. This report covers everything I ran, the numbers, and what I'm choosing.

Normal light = even lighting and few fingers in the way. Hard conditions = strong colour casts, glare, more fingers, blur and a noisy camera.

No single open-source project does the whole job. None of them checks the user's moves live. The pipeline I'm building combines the best piece for each step and adds the move tracking myself.

How I tested

Test imagesA renderer I wrote draws a 3D cube in any scrambled state (states come from our own cube model) on cluttered backgrounds, with random lighting, colour casts, glare, fingers, blur, camera noise and JPEG compression. Every sticker position is known exactly, so every result can be scored automatically.
Real photosdwalton76's published photos of one real cube (6 faces, held by a robot) as a real-world check.
FairnessEach project's own code ran with its own settings. Where a fixed size limit made a project fail on our frame sizes (qbr, ephynes), I also ran a multi-scale variant that retries on scaled copies of the frame, labelled "our tweak". The C# project could not run here (no .NET/Unity), so I ported its detection and colour code to Python line by line with all original thresholds.
ScoringSticker finding counts only if all 9 stickers land on the right stickers in the right order. Colours count whole cubes with all 54 stickers right. Tracking counts a trial correct only if it names exactly what happened.
MachineApple M1 Pro laptop.

4 · How much of the cube the camera must see

For 300 random cubes I checked, using our cube model, whether every possible move (and every pair of moves) leaves a different picture on the visible sides.

% of cubes where the move can't be identified (lower is better)

The camera must see 3 sides (Top, Front, Right) during the solve. With the front alone, turns of the back are invisible; with Top + Front, two quick moves are confused half the time; with 3 sides every move and every pair is identifiable.

7 · The ephynes C# project (AR-Optimal-Rubiks-Cube-solver)

Because our end product runs on XREAL glasses with a C# (Unity) app, I checked this C# AR solver in detail. It is a Unity port of AndroidSteve's "Rubik Cube Wizard", built for a Meta 2 headset study.

Areaephynes (C#)Best option foundVerdict
Following moves during the solveNone: advances whenever a stable face reappears; never checks the moveYOLO + matcher: 98.7% correct, 0 wrongMissing
Finding a hand-held face31.5% right order (38.5% with multi-scale); grid often rotatedYOLO: 100% foundBest classical, not enough
Corner view (3 sides)4.0%YOLO: 100%No
Colours52% / 16% cubes right98% / 88.7%No
Solver20.7 moves (two-phase, maxDepth 25)kociemba 20.7Same
Speed per frame2.2 msYOLO 15 ms (M1 Pro GPU)Faster
  • No move checking: during the solve it moves to the next instruction whenever the camera sees a stable face again (AppStateMachine.cs), so a wrong move goes unnoticed and nothing is re-planned.
  • Rotated grid: it orders the 9 stickers from whichever corner of a sticker is highest in the image, so a small tilt left or right can rotate the grid by 90°. Allowing any rotation, it finds the face in 55.5% of frames, versus 31.5% in the right order.
  • Colour check disabled: its "exactly 9 of each colour" step is commented out and would crash if enabled, so colours are named one face at a time.
  • Worth reusing: its Unity structure (camera into OpenCvSharp, state machine, Kalman filter) and possibly its C# two-phase solver, once the solver's licence is confirmed (it was translated from a project with no licence file).

It does not perform better. It has the best classical face detector and is very fast, but it can't check moves, which is the core feature I need.

8 · Hardware findings (XREAL One Pro + Beam Pro)

  • Camera: our glasses have the XREAL Eye camera add-on, so the camera sees what the wearer sees, which is the ideal view for this app. XREAL's documentation confirms the Eye is the camera apps can use on the One series.
  • Compute: the Beam Pro is an Android 14 device with a Snapdragon 6 Gen 1 and two 50 MP rear cameras. YOLO can run on it in Unity through the Inference Engine (Sentis) from an ONNX export; speed on this mid-range chip still needs measuring.
  • SDK: the One Pro uses XREAL SDK 3.x (camera class XREALRGBCameraTexture, YUV_420_888 frames), not the older NRSDK our Unity scaffold was written against.

What I'm going with

  1. Solver: kociemba.
  2. Finding the cube: a YOLO pose model for both the scan and tracking.
  3. Reading 3 sides: the 7-corner-point method.
  4. Colours: centre rebalance with rubiks-color-resolver as fallback; re-calibrated from the last frame during tracking.
  5. Move tracking: compare against every predicted move; wait when unsure, rescan only when lost.
  6. Rollout: Phase 1 runs on a laptop with its webcam (Python pipeline + browser view). Phase 2, once Phase 1 works, moves it to our XREAL glasses with the Eye camera, with the app in C# on the Beam Pro.
Limits. Almost all images are synthetic, so real-world numbers will be lower; YOLO in particular has only seen synthetic frames and must be fine-tuned on a few hundred real photos of our cubes. The only real photos were 6 photos of one robot-held cube. Tracking was tested on single before/after frames; the real app will also average several steady frames.
